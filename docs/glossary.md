# Glossary

The single source of this domain's terms. Code, tests, events, and UI use
these terms and no others — do not introduce a synonym for a term already
listed here.

| Term                               | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                               | Is not                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Phrase**                         | One French/English pair. A unit of learning — the original one, and since schema v7 not the only one: a **Passage** is drilled too. Entity local to its Deck; has an `id` so the correction and edit screens can address it under shuffle.                                                    | Not a "word" — the directive was corrected: these are phrases. Not a flashcard: it has no schedule, no score.                                                                                                                            |
| **French text** / **English text** | The two halves of a Phrase. Fields `french`, `english`. French is the side that is drilled three times; English is the side spoken once.                                                                 | Not `front`/`back` (no card metaphor), not `target`/`native` (generality not yet earned — refused deliberately for a single-user, single-language app; a second language is an additive migration, not a reason to provision now).       |
| **Deck**                           | A named collection of Phrases for one context (`home`, `friends`, `work`, `formal`, `climbing`). The thing the owner called a "list". An aggregate root and a unit of persistence — one of two, beside a **Passage**.                       | Not `List` — collides fatally with the data structure; a type named `List` holding an array is two concepts in one word. Not `Context` — collides with React Context and general programming vocabulary. Not an Anki deck: no scheduler. |
| **Passage**                        | One long-form French text the user types or pastes — a paragraph, a page, a letter — practised by reading it aloud rather than by recall. An aggregate root and a unit of persistence, beside a Deck: `{ id, name, text }`, its own `passages` store, and its own field in the `Library` envelope (schema v7), so it exports, backs up, syncs and merges exactly as a Deck does. Single-language: it has no English half, is never translated, and no Phrase Candidate is ever proposed for one. Drilled over its derived **Lines**. Their own word for one is a "long form entry", and **Long form** is the label on the screen that lists them — recorded here for the same reason the Deck row records "the thing the owner called a list". | Not a Deck — a Deck is a collection of Phrases, a Passage is one text, and neither contains the other. Not a Phrase: no pair, no English side, and not the Phrase Cadence. Not a Mix, which holds the ids of Decks and owns no text of its own. Not a scheduled or scored thing — it carries no more progress framing than a Deck does: no score, no next-due date, nothing kept between runs. |
| **Line**                           | One sentence-sized piece of a Passage, derived from its text by `splitPassageIntoLines` (`src/domain/line.ts`): split on a sentence terminator (`.`, `!`, `?`, `…`) followed by whitespace or the end of the text, and on any newline; then any piece longer than `LINE_MAX_CHARS` (300) split again at the last whitespace inside the cap. The unit of audio and of progress inside a Passage's Drill; id `${passage.id}#${index}`, the index running over the final list. **Derived, never persisted and never authored** — editing the Passage's text re-derives every Line, which is why a stale Line cannot exist. It exists because `server/app.js` caps one text-to-speech request at `TTS_MAX_TEXT_CHARS` (2,000 characters) and its body at 8,000 bytes: pages of text cannot be one Clip, so a Passage has to be cut into Clip-sized pieces before any audio exists at all. 300 sits well inside both caps, keeps one Clip near one breath-group, and bounds the Line pause at 19.5 s. | Not a line of the source text — the split is on sentence ends as well as on newlines, so one typed paragraph of three sentences is three Lines and a sentence the user wrapped by typing Return mid-way is two. Not a Phrase: no id of its own on disk, no English half, no pair, and nothing of theirs is lost when one stops existing. Not a Step, which is one element of a Cadence rather than the thing a Cadence is built for. |
| **Cadence**                        | The fixed playback pattern applied to one unit. For a Phrase: **FR · pause · FR · pause · EN · pause · FR · pause**. For a **Line**: **FR · pause** — one reading, then room to say it back, because a Phrase is short and memorised while a page is read. Defined as pure data (a sequence of Steps), not as behaviour. The heart of the product. A Line's pause is clamped by its own ceiling, `PASSAGE_PAUSE_MAX_MS` (20 s), because the Phrase ceiling `PAUSE_MAX_MS` (5 s) is shorter than it takes to say a 300-character sentence, and a pause the user cannot finish speaking in defeats the only thing a pause is for.         | Not "phrasing" (the owner's word — collides with Phrase itself), not a setting: it is fixed in v1.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Step**                           | One element of the Cadence: either an Utterance (speak one **Statement** — the French text or the English text of a Phrase, or the text of a Line) or a Pause (silence of a stated duration).                                                                   |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Rep**                            | One unit played through the full Cadence, start to finish — a Phrase, or one **Line** of a Passage. The unit of progress inside a Drill. It also carries every **Statement** that must have a Clip before it can play, which is what lets a single readiness rule serve both kinds without knowing which it holds.                                                                                                        | Not a "review" — no scheduling connotation wanted.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Drill**                          | One run-through: an ordered sequence of Reps over the Phrases of one Deck or one Mix, or over the **Lines** of one Passage, with a position, playing/paused/stopped state. In-memory only; not persisted.                                      | Not a "session" — that word is taken by the workflow system and by web infrastructure.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Shuffle**                        | Random reordering (Fisher–Yates) of the Phrases at Drill start. A property of the Drill, not of the Deck: the Deck keeps its author order. Never applied to a Passage: a page read out of order is not the text, so a Passage's Drill is always in author order and there is no switch that says otherwise.                                                               |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Mix**                            | A named, saved selection of Decks, drilled as one run: their Phrases pooled in Mix order and shuffled. The owner's word is "join"; corrected deliberately. An entity — `id`, `name`, and the **ids** of its Decks (`deckIds`), persisted beside them (schema v4, T059). Saving, renaming, editing or deleting a Mix never touches a Deck, and deleting a Deck never touches a Mix: a Deck deleted out from under one is skipped when the Mix resolves, and its id is kept so a restored Deck rejoins it. | Not a merge: the source Decks are untouched. **No longer ephemeral** — it was, until T059, when the owner asked to save, edit and delete Mixes. Not a copy of its Decks: it holds ids, never Phrases, so an edited Deck changes every Mix that names it. A selection started *without* being saved is not a Mix — it leaves nothing behind, and is simply a Drill over several Decks. |
| **Scan**                           | One photo of handwritten phrases submitted for reading. Input to the Import port.                                                                                                                        |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Draft Phrase**                   | A French/English pair proposed by the vision model from a Scan. Exists only on the correction screen; becomes a Phrase only when confirmed into a Deck. Nothing reaches storage un-reviewed.             | Not a Phrase — the distinction is the whole point of the correction screen.                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Phrase Candidate**                | A machine-proposed rendering of the other side of a Phrase, offered when adding one (T057): the user types one side, one or more Candidates are proposed for the other, optionally labelled with the register they represent (`tu`, `vous`, `formal`, `casual`) when the phrase genuinely supports more than one natural rendering — one unlabelled Candidate is a correct, complete result, not a degraded one. Exists only on the Add sheet; each one the user accepts becomes its own Phrase, into a Deck chosen at accept time. Nothing reaches storage un-reviewed. | Not a Draft Phrase — a Draft Phrase comes from a Scan and already has both sides; a Phrase Candidate proposes only the side not yet typed. Not a Phrase — no `id`, does not exist until accepted. |
| **Statement**                      | One text in one language — `{ text, lang }` — and exactly what one Clip is the audio of: the two components of a Clip's content address (`provider\|modelId\|voiceId\|lang\|text`, `computeClipHash` in `src/adapters/storage/clip-cache.ts`) that are not the **Voice**. A Phrase has two Statements, its French and its English; a Line has one. It is what a **Rep** carries, and carrying it is what lets one readiness rule — every Statement of the unit has a cached Clip in some voice — decide a Phrase and a Line without knowing which it holds. | Not a Clip: a Clip is the cached audio of one Statement in one Voice, so one Statement has as many Clips as there are voices it has been rendered in, and losing all of them loses no Statement. Not an Utterance, which is a Step inside a Cadence — the same Statement is uttered three times in one Phrase Cadence and is still one Statement. |
| **Voice**                          | The text-to-speech voice a Clip is rendered in: `provider`, `modelId`, `voiceId` — two thirds of a Clip's content address. Chosen from a curated catalogue (`voice-catalogue.ts`); the one the user has chosen is the **pinned voice**, and it decides exactly one thing (T067): what the NEXT **Statement** with no audio — a Phrase's side, or a Line — is generated in. It rides in the `Library` envelope as its own named field, so a new phone restores the preference; two devices disagreeing resolve by last writer wins, and an envelope carrying none never clears one. | Not a property of the app, of a Deck, or of a Phrase — it belongs to each Clip. Not something that can invalidate audio: re-pinning leaves every existing Clip playable, and playback prefers the pinned voice only where a Clip exists in it. Not a credential, and not the rest of the `settings` record, none of which travels. |
| **Clip**                           | The cached audio rendering of one **Statement** — one text in one language — in one voice. Content-addressed by a hash of `provider\|modelId\|voiceId\|lang\|text`, so a given voice-and-statement is generated exactly once, ever, and an edited Phrase or a retyped Passage points at a different hash — staleness is impossible by construction. **Its voice is a property of the Clip, not of the app (T067):** one Statement may hold Clips in several voices at once, they all stay playable, and pinning another **Voice** invalidates none of them. Derived, regenerable; never user data. The cache holding them is **bounded** and evicts (see **Eviction**). | Not stored data in the export sense — excluded from `Library`/`exportAll()`. Not a Phrase's or a Passage's identity: losing every Clip loses nothing but generation time. No longer orphaned by a voice change — that was true until T067 and cost a whole library's regeneration for a preference. |
| **Eviction**                       | Deleting cached Clips to keep the clip cache under its ceiling (200 MB, T036), least recently *played* first — `get()` counts as playing, the readiness sweep's `has()` does not. Runs on any `put` that crosses the line **and on the first index build of every launch (T076)**, because a phone that arrived over the ceiling and generates nothing would otherwise never shrink. Exists because on iOS storage pressure is itself an eviction trigger, so an unbounded audio cache is on course to make the browser discard the whole origin, Phrases and Passages included. It can reach the `clips` and `clipMeta` stores and nothing else, structurally. | Not a deletion of anything of theirs: it cannot touch a Phrase, a Deck, a Mix, a Passage or a Tombstone, and refusing to be able to is the point. It reaches Lines only as their Clips, which is no loss: a Line is re-derived from its Passage on the next read. Not the same as an **orphan**; eviction removes Clips that are still wanted, to buy room. |
| **Orphan**                         | A record whose referent is gone. Two of them, one relation: an **orphaned Clip** is audio nothing points at any more (an edited Phrase, or a Passage retyped so that Line no longer exists, points at a different hash; **not** a re-pinned voice, which since T067 leaves every Clip in use), and an **orphaned index row** is a `clipMeta` row whose Clip is gone. Eviction no longer makes them — the Clip and its row go in one transaction (T078) — so what is left is a phone carrying rows the old two-step eviction orphaned, and audio the browser dropped on its own. Both are derived, so both are deleted on sight: the index is reconciled against the audio, in both directions, before the ceiling is applied to it (T076, docs/scale.md §8). | Never anything of theirs: a Phrase, Deck, Mix, Passage or Tombstone cannot be orphaned, because nothing the user authors is derived from anything else. A **Line** is derived, but it is never a record — it is recomputed from its Passage on every read, so it leaves nothing behind to orphan, only the Clips above. Not a harmless leak either — an orphaned index row is charged against the **Eviction** ceiling and makes `has()` promise audio that is gone. |
| **Session token**                  | The device's identity on the server (T050, `docs/server.md`): an opaque 32-byte random token, issued by `POST /api/login` and held only client-side (`localStorage`); the server stores only its SHA-256 hash, keyed to a `users` row, in a `sessions` table with a 30-day expiry. Sent as `Authorization: Bearer <token>` on every `/api/*` call; identifies which library is theirs, nothing more. | Not a JWT — it carries no claims and cannot be verified offline; every use is a database lookup, which is what makes revocation (delete the row) free. Not a provider API key — the device never holds one of those. Not the deleted **Library key** (below) it replaces: that was self-issued and unauthenticated; this is issued by the server after a username/password check. |
| **Library key** *(retired, T050)*  | The device's pre-login identity (T041–T049): 64 lowercase hex characters generated on-device and shown in the open in Settings. Replaced by **Session token** above — kept here only so old references (commit history, prior task notes) resolve to a real term. | Not in use. Do not reintroduce. |
| **Diagnostic report**              | The plain-text snapshot the Diagnostics screen shows and its one control copies whole (`docs/design.md` §3.7): build sha/timestamp, pinned voice, Clips ready of total Phrases **and** Clips ready of total Passage Lines as two separate lines (counts only), storage usage/quota, last sync, and the last several entries from the on-device error log. The two readiness populations are never merged and are printed even at zero — `collectDiagnostics` takes a **required** `passageStore` for exactly that reason, since one number cannot tell "their Passage has no audio" from "their Deck has no audio", and that is the distinction the person reading their pasted report needs. Built so a remote, non-technical user can answer "it's not working" without guessing. | Not a support ticket, not sent anywhere automatically — stays on-device until pasted into a message by hand. Not a log of everything: the error log it draws from is a bounded ring buffer (`ERROR_LOG_CAP`), not an unbounded history.  |
| **Backup age**                     | How long since their Library was last safe somewhere other than this phone: the elapsed whole days since the more recent of the last successful server sync and the last backup file the user exported themselves (`domain/backup-age.ts`). Has four levels — `never`, `fresh` (under 7 days), `aging` (7–29), `overdue` (30+) — and the level, not the number, decides how loudly the app states it and how far it follows them. Because sync runs after every save, an age that climbs is the app's only visible signal that **sync has been failing silently**. | Not a reminder and not a nudge: it is a stated fact with no dismiss control, it never repeats, and it never interrupts. Not a measure of how old their phrases are. Not affected by Clips, which are derived and regenerable. |
| **Tombstone**                      | The record that a Deck, a Mix or a Passage was deleted, and when: `{ id, kind, deletedAt }` with `kind` one of `'deck' \| 'mix' \| 'passage'`, kept after the thing it names is gone and carried in the `Library` envelope (T060). `kind` is not decoration, and since schema v7 it carries three aggregates rather than two through one id namespace: a Tombstone must delete exactly the aggregate it was written for, never a Deck that happens to share an id with a deleted Passage. It is what lets sync merge two devices instead of overwriting one with the other — without it, a merge cannot tell "the user deleted this" from "this device has never seen it", so every device still holding the Deck pushes it back and a delete never sticks. Never garbage-collected: any expiry window is also the window after which an offline device resurrects their data. | Not an undo — it holds nothing of the Deck's contents and cannot restore it. Not a soft delete: the Deck record itself is really gone from the `decks` store, and a Tombstone is a separate, tiny record in its own store. |
| **Split id**                       | The id given to the second and later Deck, Mix or Passage holding an id another already holds: `${id}-2`, `${id}-3`, …, skipping any the library already carries (T090, `duplicate-ids.ts`). Applied by `importAll` and `updateAll`, because `decks`, `mixes` and `passages` are keyed by `id` and one `put` per record would otherwise let the second overwrite the first — a whole Deck, or a whole Passage, gone from disk with nothing said. Deterministic, so repeated merges converge; collision-free, so the repair cannot itself overwrite a record. Both records keep their content and their names, so the user sees two Decks and merges or deletes them themselves. | Not a new Deck — it is one of two records that were already there. Not a merge and not a refusal: `mergeLibraries` keeps both (T086) and the store splits them, because refusing a restore file that may be them only copy costs them everything in it to avoid a defect whose cost is one tap. Not applied to a Tombstone, whose id names the record it deletes. |
| **Sync**                           | One round-trip that leaves this device's library — Decks with their Phrases, Mixes, Passages, Tombstones and the pinned voice — and the server's holding the same thing: pull, merge, write the merge back locally, push it. Runs by itself (T034, `docs/sync.md`) — at launch, after every change (debounced), on reconnect, and when the app is backgrounded. Never destructive: a push carries the merge of both sides, so it can add records to the server copy and never remove one. | Not a backup — a backup is a file the user exports and keeps (`docs/design.md` §3.6); a Sync is continuous and has no artefact the user holds. Not an upload: it reads before it writes, always. Not something the user starts — there is no sync control anywhere in the app. |
| **Sync Baseline**                  | The last whole-library snapshot — Decks, Mixes, Passages and Tombstones — this device and the server are known to have agreed on: what this device pushed and the server accepted. Held per-device, never sent anywhere. It is the common ancestor `mergeLibraries` needs to tell "changed here" from "changed there", which is what lets two devices edit different Phrases of one Deck, or one device edit a Deck while the other edits a Passage, without either losing a record (T034). It is also causal rather than chronological: a Tombstone deletes only a record unchanged from it (T070), which is why a restore from file sets it EMPTY — nothing on this device is agreed with the server afterwards, so every restored record outranks a deletion the server still holds (T072). | Not a backup, not history — one snapshot, replaced on every successful push, and emptied by a restore as one operation with the restore's own write (T081). Not user data: derived and regenerable, and losing it costs the next merge its precision (it falls back to whole-record last-write-wins) and nothing else — which is why one written under a schema version this build does not read is treated as absent rather than refused (T081). |
| **Refused write**                  | A write to this phone's own storage that the device would not take: a `QuotaExceededError` when the origin is full, an IndexedDB transaction iOS aborted under memory pressure, a `versionchange` from another surface. Distinct from anything to do with the server. Every write in the app is optimistic — the screen changes first — so a Refused write is the one event that can leave a Phrase or a Passage on screen that is not on disk, and it always produces two things together (T069): the screens are re-read from the stores so they show what is really held, and the **Write failure notice** states which change did not survive. A Refused write is never announced to the sync engine; there is nothing to send. | Not a sync failure — sync has its own line, which stays quiet because a sync that has not happened yet has lost nothing. Not something the error log alone answers for: the Diagnostics screen is not a place the user goes. |
| **Write failure notice**           | The single alert in the app (`src/ui/WriteFailureNotice.tsx`): one sentence naming what did not save, one standing sentence saying the phone may be out of space and that the screen now shows what is really stored, and a control to put it away. Fixed to the top of whatever screen the user is on, including a running Drill. | Not a toast — it does not time out. Not the sync line, which is standing information about the server and is deliberately quiet. Not one per failure: the most recent one is shown, because two of these at once is one condition, not two. |
| **Route hold**                     | A second, dedicated `<audio>` element (`src/adapters/audio/route-hold.ts`, its element declared in `index.html` beside `unlock-audio`) looping an inaudible but not digitally-silent 44.1 kHz 16-bit mono PCM source for the whole of a Drill: started by the same Start-Drill tap that unlocks the shared element, re-started on Resume, released when the Drill stops. It exists because a Drill's steady state is a second or two of Clip followed by a Pause of 1.5–5 s (up to 20 s in a Passage's Drill, where the gap it has to bridge is longer still) during which the shared element is paused and its source revoked (`clip-player.ts` `finish()`), and a Bluetooth A2DP route that goes idle in that gap is re-acquired with the head of the next Clip already gone — heard in a car as choppiness. What it holds is the phone's audio output route, nothing else. | Not part of the Cadence: not a Step, no pedagogy, and it never changes a Pause's duration. Not the Wake Lock, which holds the screen. Not audible content — its samples are ±1 LSB and if it can be heard at all that is a defect. Not a confirmed fix: whether a looping second element keeps an A2DP route alive on iOS Safari is the assumption it exists to measure, which is why the Diagnostic report states how long it was held and how long it actually played. |
| **Generation suspension**          | The Clip-generation queue issuing nothing for the whole of a Drill — from the Start tap until the run stops, a Pause included. Mechanically `GenerationQueue.suspend()` / `resume()` (`src/adapters/audio/generation-queue.ts`), idempotent boolean writes taken from two places (the Start tap, and the effect that watches the running phase in `DrillScreen.tsx`) and released from one. The gate sits at `requestClip`'s loop head and again at `generateOne`'s head, so a suspended queue issues no request, computes no content hash and reads no IndexedDB. It exists because four concurrent HTTPS fetches, the MP3 bodies behind them and the writes that follow compete with the audio the user is listening to on cellular — and none of that work is for the Drill that is playing, since only ready Reps enter one. Bounded, not instantaneous: at most `maxConcurrent` (4) already-issued attempts land after it is taken, with no retries behind them. | Not a **Pause**, which is a Step of the Cadence and is silence the user hears. Not a **Route hold** — a hold keeps something alive, this stops something happening; the two words point opposite ways. Not iOS suspending media on screen lock or backgrounding, which is the screen's own **interrupted** state (`DrillScreen.tsx`) and shares nothing with this but the word. Not a cancellation: nothing in flight is aborted and nothing queued is dropped. |
